## 1. The short answer

The field has moved. In frames dated 1 to 13 October 2026, Alex Hormozi, Amy Porterfield, Mel Robbins, Codie Sanchez, Marie Forleo, The Diary of a CEO, and Leila Hormozi in half her frames, use **small, white, lower-case or sentence-case captions of one to four words, with a soft shadow and no outline**. The loud all-caps look with a neon word and an emoji is what Sallie's Shorts use today, in at least three variants in two weeks. The man that look is named after does not use it in any of the four frames seen.

Four styles are the safest fit for a business coach to women founders:

1. **Clean** (the house default). Small sentence-case white captions in Montserrat, natural colour, light cuts.
2. **Editorial** (the brand signature). Bodoni Moda headlines on deep green and cream. Serif for titles, sans for running captions.
3. **Teaching** (frameworks and lists). Numbered title cards and a side panel, for her "five constraints" kind of video.
4. **Conversation** (interviews). Caption on a plate so it reads over any background, name lower thirds, almost no zoom.

One optional fifth, **Bold**, is a restrained version of big captions for testing a hook on cold viewers. A "moody" look is covered in section 6: a mild version is safe, a strong one is not.

Avoid: neon word highlights, auto emoji, sound effects, a different caption template on every clip, captions in the bottom third, borders around the picture, AI-made pictures or b-roll, and cutting out every breath.

## 4. The evidence, by platform

### Instagram

- A Reel runs 3 seconds to 3 minutes. Instagram calls 15 to 60 seconds the best fit for most formats. Source: https://about.instagram.com/blog/tips-and-tricks/how-to-make-instagram-reels, reported 28 Sep 2026 by https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-reels-creation-tips-in-new-guide/831551/. The tool's 15 to 90 seconds fits.
- Opening text in the first frame works like a headline. Captions are a separate thing from text overlays, and Instagram advises both. Same source.
- Instagram makes Reels less visible when they are low-resolution, watermarked, muted, **bordered**, or mostly text. Source: https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained (31 May 2023). So: no black bars around a wide clip, no tool watermark, no text-only slides.
- The strongest predictions are whether a viewer will reshare, watch to the end, or like. Same source.
- **Safe zones.** Keep text and logos out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% at each side of a 9:16 picture. Source: Meta's ads guide for Reels, https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels, read 2026-10-13. It is written for ads. Ordinary Reels carry the same kind of interface over the picture. Sallie's upright caption look at about 80% down sits inside the bottom zone.
- Original work is favoured. Accounts that mostly repost lose recommendation. A creator's own footage counts as original. Sources: https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram (30 Apr 2026) and https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines.
- Adam Mosseri in September 2026: aim for engagement rate and not raw views, and test with trial Reels. Source: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/ (24 Sep 2026).
- **Not verified:** Mosseri's essay at the end of 2025 saying that an unpolished look now signals a real person. I recall wide coverage but could not fetch a source today.

### LinkedIn

- Limits: 3 seconds to 15 minutes, up to 5 GB, 256x144 to 4096x2304, aspect 1:2.4 to 2.4:1, 10 to 60 frames a second, up to 30 Mbit. ProRes may upload and then fail to process. Keep the edges free of text and logos. Source: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548372, read 2026-10-13.
- There is a full-screen upright video player. A video cannot be posted into it directly: any posted video may appear there. Wider videos may be cropped. Viewers can turn closed captions on. Source: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6290168.
- Video creation up 27% and views up 36% in 2025, by LinkedIn's own figures as cited in https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-statistics-business/ (29 Jun 2026). Uploads grew by double digits for a third quarter in a row in early 2026 (https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/, 14 Jul 2026).
- More supply, fewer views each. Across 1.3 million posts from 16,645 company pages, video engagement rose from 5.6% to 6.0%, average views per video fell 36%, and pages posted twice as many videos. Documents (7.0%) and multi-image posts (6.45%) still beat video. Source: https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin (16 Mar 2026). Buffer, on over a million posts, found carousels earn nearly three times the engagement of video: https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-algorithm/ (19 Dec 2025).
- LinkedIn staff, as reported by Buffer: the feed favours knowledge and advice from people with expertise, for a specific professional audience. Engagement bait is penalised. Same Buffer source.
- **Not verified:** a LinkedIn-published ideal length for video, and the share of LinkedIn video watched with the sound off.

### YouTube

- Shorts can be up to 3 minutes. From 31 March 2025 a Shorts view counts each time the Short starts to play, with no minimum watch time. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070, read 2026-10-13. So Shorts view counts are not comparable with long-video views.
- Upload settings: MP4, H.264, 8 Mbit for 1080p at 24 to 30 frames a second, 12 Mbit at 48 to 60; AAC-LC, 48 kHz, stereo 384 kbit; BT.709 colour. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171. The tool's rough cut at 8 Mbit already matches.
- Captions as a file widen the audience (deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, other languages). Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734796. No long video seen in the field burns captions in. The tool's `.srt` is the right form for long video.
- Channels whose videos look template-made or mass-produced can lose monetisation under the "inauthentic content" rule, renamed on 15 July 2025. A shared intro and outro with different content is allowed. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392. A house preset is fine. A slideshow factory is not.
- Colour correction, filters, captions, sharpening and upscaling need no "altered content" label. A realistic generated scene does. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491.
- Teaching content holds viewers best. Across 13 million videos, educational videos had the highest engagement, and viewers watched about half of a video under five minutes. Source: Wistia's State of Video 2026, https://wistia.com/about/state-of-video (22 Apr 2026). Wistia hosts business video and is not YouTube, so read it as a signal about business viewers.

### Captions in general

- Advice and tips are the kind of video people most expect captions on (55% of 5,616 US adults, April 2019). 41% of videos could not be followed without sound. Source: Verizon Media and Publicis Media, as reported by https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/ (19 Jul 2019).
- The often-repeated figure of 85% of video watched without sound comes from a few publishers' own Facebook numbers in 2016. It is not a platform figure. Source: https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/ (17 May 2016).
- **Not verified today:** the same Verizon study's figures of 69% watching with sound off in public and 80% more likely to finish a captioned video.
- Reading limits used by professionals: 42 characters a line, two lines, up to 20 characters a second for adults. Source: Netflix's English style guide, https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-Timed-Text-Style-Guide. Short social captions sit far inside these limits.
- **I found no independent measurement** that word-by-word captions beat phrase captions, or that any caption colour raises watch time. Every such claim I saw came from a company selling a caption tool.

### Trust: what reads as untrustworthy or dated to this audience

- **Over-editing.** Hootsuite's 2026 trends report says brands are moving away from over-polished content and that small stumbles now signal a real person: "Over-editing is out" (https://www.hootsuite.com/research/social-trends). A trend report, not a measurement.
- **Undisclosed AI.** In a Meltwater and YouGov survey, 28% of consumers accepted generative AI in influencer content, and 59% said hidden AI use would lower their trust in a brand. Source: an article sponsored by Meltwater, https://www.socialmediatoday.com/spons/influencer-marketing-has-an-ai-trust-problem/831579/ (5 Oct 2026). Sample size not given.
- **The "guru" look.** One commentary argues that heavy fonts, neon strokes, flying emoji and constant sound effects now mark a video as marketing-guru content, and that plain white text with a brand-colour accent suits consulting and other high-priced services. Source: https://joyspace.ai/hormozi-editing-style-2026-analysis (10 Dec 2025). It is one vendor's opinion and cites no study. The October frames agree with it.
- **Polish that distances.** The producer's review above doubted a very polished set for founder talks. One reviewer's view.
- **Inconsistency.** No source measured this. It is my judgement from her own frames: three caption looks and three thumbnail looks in two weeks read as three different shows.

## 5. Presets to build

Numbers marked (S) come from a source above. Numbers marked (O) are what the field's frames show. Numbers marked (P) are my proposal: a starting point to test, not a measured optimum.

A note on how a preset travels. The repo's README (section "Unedited video") says the Final Cut file holds no title or graphic, because a title there is only the name of a template on one Mac, and this Mac's ffmpeg cannot draw text. So the caption and title settings below need a home: a title template made once on this Mac, or another way to draw text. The cut, crop, loudness and output settings need nothing new.

### Shared by every preset

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Caption typeface | Montserrat, weight 600 (her site's text font) (P) |
| Caption fill | White `#FFFFFF`, or cream `#F4EFE6` (P) |
| Caption edge | Soft shadow: black at 60 to 70%, blur about 0.5% of frame height, no offset. No outline (O for the kind, P for the numbers) |
| Words per caption | 2 to 4, one line, at most about 20 characters (O) |
| Upright caption position | Centre at 62 to 64% down. Nothing below 65% (S: Meta safe zone) |
| Upright title position | Between 14% and 30% down. 6% clear at each side (S) |
| Emoji, sound effects | Off |
| Second colour in running captions | None (O) |
| Logo | Small, top right, inside the safe zone (O) |
| Long video (16:9) | No burned captions. Upload the `.srt` (S, O) |
| Loudness | Podcast files: -16 LUFS, give or take 1, true peak -1 dB (S: https://podcasters.apple.com/support/893-audio-requirements). Social and YouTube: -14 LUFS is the usual working figure, not verified against a platform page |
| Upright output | 1080 by 1920, H.264, AAC at 128 kbit or more (S: Meta ads guide). No bars, no watermark (S) |
| Clip length | Reels 15 to 60 seconds first, up to 90 (S). Stories parts of at most 60, the tool's own limit |

Notes on these:

- **Bodoni needs care at small sizes.** Its thin strokes break up on a phone. Use it for titles and single words at 5% of frame height or more, in weight 600 or 700. Use Montserrat for anything that runs. This is why "Editorial" is serif titles over sans captions, and not serif captions throughout, although Codie Sanchez and Marie Forleo do run serif captions.
- **Pause lengths and punch-in sizes are rules of thumb.** No measured figure exists for them. The breakdown pages report 1 to 3 seconds between cuts for the old Hormozi look and 3 to 8 for Leila Hormozi, with pauses left in.
- **The same preset should set the long video's lower titles**, so the 16:9 episode and its clips match.

### Optional fifth: **Bold** (for testing a hook on cold viewers)

Montserrat 800, all capitals, 5% of frame height, 2 to 3 words, white with a soft shadow, the one stressed word on a lavender box with deep green text. No neon, no emoji, no bounce, no outline. Leila Hormozi's pink-box frame is the model (O). Use it for the first 3 seconds, or for a trial Reel, which Instagram offers for exactly this kind of test (https://creators.instagram.com/create-engaging-content). Keep "Clean" for the rest of the clip.

## 6. What to avoid, and why

| Avoid | Reason | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| All-caps captions with neon yellow, green or red words, thick outline, bounce | The field's leaders have left it, its namesake among them. It clashes with deep green, cream and lavender. One commentary says it now reads as guru content | Observed 13 Oct 2026; joyspace.ai (opinion) |
| Auto emoji and auto key-word colours | They come from a switch in a tool (OpusClip's "AI Emoji", https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/ai-emojis-keywords), not from the brand. None of the 11 peers' frames has an emoji | Observed |
| A different caption template per clip | Reads as different shows. Neither YouTube's rule nor Instagram's originality rule objects to a consistent house style | My judgement; YouTube Help |
| Captions lower than 65% down | Covered by the Reels interface | Meta ads guide |
| A wide clip set in black bars, a tool watermark, text-only slides | Instagram shows these less | Instagram, 31 May 2023 |
| Generated b-roll, generated backgrounds, heavy AI retouching, thumbnails included | 59% say hidden AI lowers trust. A realistic generated scene needs a label on YouTube | Meltwater and YouGov (sponsored); YouTube Help |
| Cutting every breath; a cut every 1 to 3 seconds; constant zoom | Reads as over-edited. Her buyers are judging whether to trust her with their business | Hootsuite 2026 (trend report); rule of thumb |
| Sound effects on cuts; music loud enough to notice | None reported for Leila Hormozi or Codie Sanchez. Not observable in frames | Choppity (reported); not verified |
| Lavender text on a light or busy picture | 2.2 to 1 on white, 1.9 to 1 on cream | Worked out from the site's colours |
| A strong "moody" grade: crushed blacks, teal and orange, heavy vignette | The dark shows (Robbins, Diary of a CEO) look dark in their sets and lighting. A bright room graded dark looks false and is hard to see on a phone outdoors. Her nearest peers are warm and neutral | Observed sets; my judgement |

**A mild "moody" look is safe** as a colour option on top of Clean or Editorial: exposure down about a quarter stop, contrast up 5 to 8%, saturation down 5 to 10%, a slightly warm white balance, vignette 10 to 15% (P). Her green wall will take it well. Treat these numbers as a first try on real footage.

## 7. Two things no preset can fix

- **The crop is too tight because the recording is.** The upright version is a full-height slice of a wide picture, so the face is as large in the slice as in the original. To match the field, frame the wide shot so the face is a quarter to a third of the picture height. (O)
- **The crop is soft because the source is small.** A 9:16 slice of a 1920 by 1080 picture is 608 pixels wide and must be enlarged 1.78 times. A slice of a 3840 by 2160 picture is 1215 wide and needs no enlarging. Recording in 4K fixes it. (Arithmetic. The repo's README, section "Unedited video", notes the same softness for small sources.)

Both belong in a short "how to record" note beside the presets.
