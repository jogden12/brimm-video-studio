After reading the first draft he decided: "make the presets combinable: job, caption style and look". Later that day he decided that the brand guide is the brand: "the website should follow the brand guide but doesnt always." Research only. Nothing here is built.

Seven other research reports (styles, captions, pacing, platforms, look and sound, Final Cut Pro and ffmpeg, other tools) were never written as files. What they found is carried in the draft and the answer, with their sources.

## The answer in a few lines

- **Jobs** (what the video is and where it goes): Full episode, Solo clip, Interview clip, Teaching clip, As filmed.
- **Caption styles** (how words in the picture look): File only, Plain, Boxed, Bold, Signature.
- **Looks** (colour): Natural, Bright, Warm, Moody.
- Each job names a default caption style and look. Any job goes with any style and any look, and ten rules that the tool applies by itself keep every pick valid.
- The brand kit is one file that is never picked. It holds the brand guide's colours (navy, fog, light gray, dew, sand, dune, mist, smoke), its typefaces (Sweet Sans Pro, and the script Sinthya for a word or two) and a logo. Until Sweet Sans Pro is at hand it lists two stand-ins, in order: Varela, then Lucida Grande.
- To build first: one test project made by hand in Final Cut Pro, then Full episode, Plain on Solo clip, Bright and Moody, Bold.

## How it was checked

Eight researchers, one writer, three checkers (facts against primary sources, feasibility against the code and Final Cut Pro's own format files, and what was missing), then a rewrite into three layers, a fourth check that tried to break every combination, and a last correction. The checkers went through 451 claims and found 75 to correct. All are corrected in `presets.md`, which lists them under "What was checked".

The same night the answer was brought to the brand guide: every brand value, a fourth look, a caption style reworked because the guide has no serif, and every width worked again. Two more checkers then went over that version, one on the brand and one on the design. They raised 18 problems. All were real, and all are corrected. The last two parts of "What was checked" say what changed each time.

## What to keep in mind

- No researcher watched a video or heard its sound. Pace, caption movement and music were not observed for anyone.
- Most numbers for short video are tool makers' rules of thumb. The answer marks each value as official, from the guide, measured, seen, a rule of thumb or a guess.
- The brand is the brand guide's, by Jason's decision of 2026-10-13. The website does not always follow it. `draft-eight-presets.md` and `report-audience.md` were left as they were written, and they describe the website's look.
- Neither of the brand's typefaces is on the Mac, and no font file is at hand. Who holds a licence is an open question. Widths were measured in Avenir, the stand-in for the tool's tests, and worked out from the letter widths that the guide's PDF records for Sweet Sans Pro. Its own font file was not measured, and no count was worked for Varela or Lucida Grande, the stand-ins for real videos.
- Sweet Sans Pro is a wide face. By an estimate from her two stored episodes, about a quarter of the house style's captions come out as one or two words and show for under the 0.83 s floor. The answer names two things to try on a phone.
- Nothing has met real footage, and no line written for Final Cut Pro has been opened in it.
