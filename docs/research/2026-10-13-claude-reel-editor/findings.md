# The reel editor review: every finding

The detail behind `review.md`. Four readers went through [nicolapocock/claude-reel-editor](https://github.com/nicolapocock/claude-reel-editor) at commit `a416076` on 2026-10-13, each from one angle, and ran nothing from it. A fifth reader tried to refute every finding of weight high or medium against the code; its verdict is beside each. Findings of weight low or note were not checked again. File and line refer to the repo at that commit.

## How it works

I read every text file at commit a416076 and ran nothing from it. The copy matches the commit's file list. It is a Claude Code skill, not a program: nine small scripts with Claude doing the judgement between them. One upright clip of one person becomes one reel of 60 seconds or less. Claude proposes cuts and graphics, a person approves on a private Artifact page, then HyperFrames draws captions and cards in headless Chrome and ffmpeg encodes. For Jason, three things matter most. It does less than his tool on framing, clip picking and unattended running: a centre crop only, no clip picking, and a person's yes for every clip. Its CapCut route is closed in the United States. But its way of drawing captions needs no libass or drawtext, so it is a fifth route beside the four in presets.md section 4.2. As written, the cut step fails on the Mac Studio: ffmpeg 9.0.2 rejects an option cut.py uses. The review page also uploads a copy of the raw clip to claude.ai, and one script reads a .env file. No text in the repo tries to direct a reviewer. Render times and output quality are the author's claims: not confirmed.

(1) ONE CLIP, RAW FILE TO REEL
Once per Mac: doctor.sh checks tools. setup_engine.sh fills engine/ with GSAP 3.13.0 (checksum checked), six sounds made with ffmpeg, and 19 HyperFrames motions. Once per person: Claude runs the interview and writes ~/reels/brand.md and ~/reels/brand.json.
a. Claude makes ~/reels/<date>-<name>/ and copies the clip in as raw.<ext> (SKILL.md:23; review_sync.py:20).
b. transcribe.py reads the clip, writes transcript.json: a flat list of words with start and end. ElevenLabs Scribe if a key is set, else local Whisper small.en through 'npx hyperframes transcribe' (transcribe.py:43-52).
c. clean_audio.sh reads the clip, writes voice.wav.
d. Claude itself looks at a frame every 0.5 s of the first and last 4 s, then proposes lines to cut, a CTA move and a graphics plan (SKILL.md:27-28).
e. Main path, the review page: tighten.py --detect writes fillers.json. Claude writes proposal.json. review_sync.py export reads transcript.json, voice.wav, fillers.json, brand.json and proposal.json, and writes reel.json and review_reel.json. Claude makes the preview files by hand with ffmpeg (no script), publishes, uploads, and sends the link. On 'build preview' Claude reads the decisions back and runs review_sync.py import, which writes edl.json (kept spans) and edit.json (captions, punch-ins, cards, hash).
   Fallback, in chat: Claude shows numbered lines and on a yes writes edl.json by hand. tighten.py rewrites edl.json and keeps edl_rough.json. After the cut, transcribe.py makes master_transcript.json, auto_edit.py drafts edit.json with captions and punch-ins, and Claude edits edit.json to mark accent words and add cards. screenshot.sh makes shots/*.png.
f. cut.py reads edl.json, the raw clip, voice.wav and brand.json. It writes cut_filter.txt and master.mp4.
g. render.sh runs build_reel.py, which reads edit.json, brand.json, master.mp4 and master_transcript.json if present, and writes proj/. HyperFrames renders proj/ to final.mp4. A loudness pass follows, then timings.log.
h. Draft at 1080p with REEL_PREVIEW=1. Claude checks frames, uploads a 540p copy and records builds/latest. On 'final', render.sh --final re-cuts master.mp4 at the brand size first (render.sh:16-20).
i. qa.py checks picture size, length against max_seconds, loudness between -15 and -12.5 LUFS, and a 95% word match if expected.txt exists. Claude looks at a frame for each card.
j. deliver.sh re-encodes final.mp4 into deliver_dir: 16 Mbit for 1080p, 35 Mbit for 4K.

(2) THE BRAND FILE
brand.json, every field found in the interview, the example and the code:
- name (no script reads it). resolution [1080,1920] or [2160,3840]. max_seconds (60).
- pacing: natural, tight or fast. speed and fillers: optional overrides (pacing.py:13-14).
- graphics: minimal, balanced or busy. Only Claude reads it (SKILL.md:35).
- colors: main and pop (required), light, soft, caption, caption_accent.
- captions: mode (word or phrase), case, size, y, style ('editorial'), tracking.
- fonts: head, body, accent, each with family, file, weight, style.
- sfx: none, subtle or lots. sfx_gain_db (default 8). music: a path (render.sh:29).
- title_outline, title_outline_px, title_y, title_tracking. require_carousel.
- grade: an ffmpeg filter string or null. deliver_dir.
- capcut, capcut_font, capcut_font_accent.
Not in the interview: speed, sfx_gain_db, music, the title_ fields, capcut_font_accent, captions.style and tracking.
brand.md holds the same brand in plain words for Claude: Account, Reference reel, Colours with a 'Never' line, Fonts, Captions, Graphics, Sound, Pacing, Call to action, and Rules. Rules is every 'never' the person has said, and Claude adds to it on each correction (SKILL.md:50). The interview is 15 questions, one at a time (setup-interview.md:15-33). The example has eight rules, such as no outlined captions and a carousel in every reel.

(3) THE REVIEW PAGE
Publish: Claude publishes reel-review.html with core.js beside it, capabilities db and assets, once; later reels reuse the link (review.md:15).
Uploaded: a 540x960 copy of the raw clip with the cleaned voice; per graphic a 180x320 frame and its image; ten seconds of sound before and after; one frame before and after the grade; each sound effect as mp4; the brand fonts; later a 540p copy of the draft.
Database: reel/current (words, proposed cut, captions, graphics, style, links), review/current (the person's decisions), builds/latest (draft link and hash).
What a person can change, in five tabs: tap or drag words to cut or keep; bring back a filler; keep or trim a pause; keep, drop or ask for a change to each graphic with a note; mark accent words, fix wording, nudge a caption by 0.1 s, merge two; switch off a sound; add notes at exact times on the draft; a note per tab; a request to change the brand; undo (60 steps); reset a tab. It saves 0.6 s after each change.
How choices come back: Claude reads review/current with ArtifactData and saves it to a file. review_sync.py import runs core.js under node, the same file the page runs, so both work out the same spans and times (review_sync.py:24-30). It prints CHANGE REQUESTED, NOTE, REVISION and BRAND CHANGE REQUESTED lines for Claude to act on. A hash of the decisions tells the page when a draft is out of date.

(4) CAPTIONS AND GRAPHICS IN THE PICTURE
HyperFrames is HeyGen's open-source tool (Apache-2.0) that turns an HTML page with timed animation into an MP4: headless Chrome draws each frame and ffmpeg encodes (https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes). build_reel.py writes proj/index.html: a 1080x1920, 30 fps stage, the cut video behind, captions and cards as divs in three layers, sound effects as audio tags, and one GSAP timeline. It also copies fonts, sounds, images and motions into proj/.
Captions: body font, white by default, at captions.y; accent words in the accent font and colour at 1.7 times (editorial) or 1.12 times; a single word at 1.35 times; a soft shadow, no box or outline; a 0.16 s pop in. Captions step aside while a card carries its own words.
Eleven card types: title, block, card, tag, cta, shot, image, sticker, page, motion, follow. Plus punch-in zooms to 1.07 over 0.22 s.
Motions fetched: four carousels, caption-camera-follow, hw-arrow, hw-underline, oversized-cursor, notification-stack, native-notification-pop, code-terminal-run, chat-message, yt-comment-card, instagram-follow, x-follow-card, count-up, animated-bar-chart, mk-line-graph, chart-story.
Render time by the repo's account: a draft is about 4 minutes (README.md:55; GUIDE.md:129; BUILD-IT-YOURSELF.md:27), or about 3 (reel-review.html:188). A 4K final 'takes longer', no figure. Two Chrome workers at low priority; no Mac is named beyond a 16 GB one (render.sh:8).

(5) CUTTING AND PACING
Presets (pacing.py:6-8):
- natural: speed 1.0; pauses over 0.6 s are cut; 0.15 s of air left each side; fillers um, uh, umm, uhm, erm, hmm, mm.
- tight: speed 1.1; over 0.25 s; 0.06 s air; the same fillers plus er, ah.
- fast: speed 1.15; over 0.2 s; 0.05 s air; fillers as tight.
Fillers: whisper-cli is run again on a 16 kHz copy with a prompt full of 'um, uh', one word per token. A token that is in the list is cut from 20 ms before it (tighten.py:43-52). Single words only.
Pauses in chat: the background level is the quietest tenth of 50 ms slices; the silence level is that plus 5 dB, at most -20 dB; ffmpeg's silencedetect finds quiet longer than the preset; the inside is cut, leaving the air (tighten.py:61-76).
Pauses on the page: a gap between two kept words longer than the preset. A pause after a question or exclamation, or over 0.8 s after a full stop, is kept by default (review_sync.py:71-73).
cut.py: one ffmpeg run trims each span, fades the sound 12 ms at each join, joins, speeds up, grades if set, scales, centre-crops, 30 fps. Final is libx264 CRF 16; preview is hardware H.264 at 12 Mbit.

(6) SOUND AND COLOUR
clean_audio.sh: mono 48 kHz. DeepFilterNet capped at 30 dB, or ffmpeg afftdn at -25 if it is not installed. A gate (threshold 0.015, 3:1, about 12 dB down). High-pass at 85 Hz. -2.5 dB at 220 Hz, +2.5 dB at 3.2 kHz, +2 dB shelf at 9 kHz. De-esser. Compressor 3:1 at -20 dB. Two-pass loudness to -14 LUFS, true peak -1.5, range 7. It warns if the background is louder than -45 dB.
render.sh repeats a one-pass loudness to -14 LUFS on the final mix. Sound effects are made from noise and sine tones, raised to a -1 dB peak, and mixed at their level plus 8 dB (for example -12 becomes 0.63).
Colour: the repo ships no grade and no numbers. brand.json 'grade' is null unless the person asks; any ffmpeg filter string there is applied in cut.py (cut.py:30-34). edl.json 'grade_from' can switch it on at a given second.

(7) THE CAPCUT ROUTE
The render is run with --capcut, so captions and sounds are left out and written to captions.json and sfx.json. CapCut's own upload script sends final.mp4 and returns upload.json. capcut_draft.py reads those and brand.json and writes draft.json: the video on the main track, each caption as a text layer (one-word captions grouped in twos and threes), accent words as patches, and sounds from CapCut's library. Claude pushes draft.json with the connector's tools and gives a link to CapCut Web. The connector is CapCut's own, added with 'claude mcp add' at capcut.com. Graphics stay baked in. Not open in the US or India.

(8) WHAT IT NEEDS
A Mac (others untested). Claude Code on a paid plan. Homebrew, ffmpeg, Node (22 or later for HyperFrames), Python 3. The HyperFrames skills and its npm package, which brings Whisper small.en on first use. Chrome, Chromium or Edge for screenshots. Optional: DeepFilterNet (through uv), yt-dlp, an ElevenLabs key, a CapCut account with its plugin and connector. Disk: about 10 GB free, and 35 GB or more for 4K (README.md:15; doctor.sh:50-51; GUIDE.md:168).

(9) WHAT IT SAYS IT CANNOT DO YET
CapCut fonts outside CapCut's list. Text animations in CapCut, added by hand. Background music. Multi-camera edits: it is for one person talking to camera. Windows and Linux. CapCut in the US and India. Also said in passing: Whisper word times can drift 0.3 s; wind and handling noise cannot be fully removed; some sites block screenshots; CapCut drops caption boxes, a push can fail over about 60 KB, and library sounds were seen to play silent in October 2026; the page cannot start a render; the rough cut on the page is not smoothed and shows graphics as boxes.

THE FOUR QUESTIONS
- Reframe a wide video? No. It expects an upright phone clip. A wide one gets a plain centre crop with no face finding (cut.py:29).
- A clip longer than a few minutes? Not well. No script refuses a long file, but the reel must come out at 60 s or less by default (qa.py:25), the guide suggests under 2 minutes, and the review page's 20 MiB asset limit holds about 3 minutes of proxy by my arithmetic. Not tested by me.
- Pick clips from a long recording? No. One clip in, one reel out.
- A person for every clip? Yes, as built: a yes before cutting, 'build preview', then 'final', with Claude and its tools in the loop. The scripts alone could be chained unattended for a plain captioned cut with no graphics, but that would be new work, and cut.py fails on this Mac's ffmpeg 9.0.2 until one option is changed.

## Safety and trust

I read all 26 text files at commit a416076 and ran none of them. I found nothing hostile: no hidden text, no encoded blobs, no attempt to steer an AI reviewer, no sudo, no posting, no secret harvesting. The original clip is copied, never moved or edited. The GSAP checksum is real and matches jsDelivr's record. The risks are those of a young tool, not a bad one. The repo is two days old: five unsigned commits by one author, no tests, no issues. It installs from moving targets: two repos' main branches, the latest HyperFrames from npm in three scripts, catalog motions from HyperFrames main, and a Whisper model with no checksum. Its doctor "check" installs things with output hidden. HyperFrames sends usage telemetry to PostHog by default and the README does not say so. The review page uploads a low-res copy of the whole raw clip and the full word-timed transcript to claude.ai, and notes typed on that page become instructions to Claude. Brand values and file names reach HTML, CSS and JS unescaped in the render template. It is safe to try on a made-up clip, in a scratch folder, with telemetry disabled first and with ElevenLabs, CapCut and page sharing left off.

## Correctness and quality

About 1,900 lines of code, written fast with Claude, with no tests. The design ideas are good. The cutting is not yet safe. Three defects matter most. 2. WHAT DOES qa.py REALLY CHECK?
Three things: the frame size equals the brand's; the length is at most max_seconds (60 by default); integrated loudness is between -15 and -12.5 LUFS. A fourth, a word match of 95% or more against expected.txt, runs only if that file exists, and nothing writes it. When it runs, a 150-word reel can lose 14 words and pass. It does not check sync, clipped words (by default), captions, text off the frame, noise, true peak, frame rate or the delivered copy. If its transcribe call fails it reads the transcript of an earlier render. PASS means size, length and loudness, no more. "Text off the edge" is checked by Claude looking at frames, which is an instruction, not code.

3. HOW DOES THE CODE READ?
Small and dense: about 1,900 lines. Flat scripts with no argument parsing, long one-line statements, one 377-line script that writes a whole web page with f-strings and globals. Copy-paste in three places. Much is hard-coded: 1080 by 1920, 30 fps, English-only small.en, a 0.66 em letter width, the author's pink and her CapCut ids. Failures are mostly raw Python tracebacks, and several calls hide the tool's error text. A few failure paths are done well (FILLERS NOT DETECTED with exit 3, exit 2 for a blank screenshot, the checksum). A good deal of what keeps it correct is prose telling Claude what to do, not code.

4. THE THREE OR FOUR WEAKEST POINTS
a. The cut itself (cut.py). It uses an option ffmpeg 9 removed, so on Homebrew's current ffmpeg, and on this Mac, it would not run. And it joins picture and sound in two separate concat filters, so they drift by up to a frame at every cut.
b. How word times are used (core.js, tighten.py, review_sync.py). The air at a cut is taken from the cut word, so words cut out of running speech leave fragments, or with the default pacing are not cut at all while their captions go. Fillers come from a second, rougher Whisper pass and are cut with a 20 ms margin. An um already in the transcript is doubled and captioned. The README's own "drifts about 0.3 s" is never bounded by code on the chat path.
c. No proof that it works. No tests, and a QA gate that checks three numbers.
d. What was approved is not bound to what is rendered. A missing review file silently means "use the proposal". Decisions are bare word numbers reused across reels on one page. Captions are dropped under cards without the page knowing. The page lists only some sounds. A 1080p final reuses the preview cut.

5. WHICH PARTS ARE SOLID ENOUGH TO LEARN FROM?
- The shared timing core: one file run by both the page and the bridge. This is the same principle as Jason's tidyTranscript rule.
- Captions and graphics drawn by a browser from one HTML template (HyperFrames). No libass or drawtext is needed. This Mac's ffmpeg 9.0.2 has neither, and has every filter these scripts do use.
- Filler detection by a Whisper prompt full of fillers, with a clear failure message.
- A pause threshold taken from each clip's own noise floor.
- The sound chain: denoise, gate, EQ, de-ess, two-pass loudnorm to -14, then a measured noise check.
- Preview small and gentle, final only on approval.
- The review page's ideas: live rough cut from a proxy, tap a word, pause chips, keep/change/drop per graphic, notes at a time in the draft. The ideas, not the present code.
Not to copy: its cutter. From a quick read of Jason's README (line 176) and a grep of cuts.js and reframe.js, not a review: his tool already cuts only in pauses ffmpeg heard, a fifth of a second from the words, puts the sound on the file's clock, joins picture and sound in one concat, and has tests. On cutting, his is the safer design.

ASKED-FOR CASES, IN SHORT
- Cuts mid-word: yes, three ways (findings on air, fillers, last word).
- Sound and picture out of step after many cuts: yes, by design of the two concats.
- Variable frame rate: handled in itself (trim by timestamp, then fps=30); the drift above still applies. Sound that starts later than the picture stays off by that amount.
- No speech: crash at cut.py. One word: works. An hour: scripts cope, the page does not, and the tool makes one reel of at most 60 s.
- A path with a space: safe. An apostrophe in the reels path, or odd characters in image names: not safe.
- A missing tool: mostly a traceback; deepFilter falls back well.
- A step run twice: tighten.py loses the rough cut; setup fetches all motions again.
- A half-written file: caught by a crash for master.mp4; an un-mixed final.mp4 can pass QA.
- Numbers that disagree: listed in one finding. Loudness, length and size do agree.
- Captions off the frame or too brief: the guard is an estimate and covers only some text; 0.12 s is the floor.
- Page and scripts on approval: five separate gaps, listed.

NOT CONFIRMED
