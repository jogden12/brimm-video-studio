# BRIMM Video Studio

> **This repository is BRIMM Video Studio, the next version of brimm-video.** The HyperFrames Mac app will do the editing; Studio does everything around it: it takes a video in, reads it, makes the app's project, takes the exports back and writes BRIMM Ops its hand-off file. The architecture and the workflows are in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-27-brimm-video-studio-architecture.md`, and the build, step by step, in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-27-brimm-video-studio-build-plan.md`. Until those steps are merged, the code and everything below describe brimm-video as it was when this repository began (the tag `brimm-video-v1` and the late-check groundwork merged after it). brimm-video itself keeps running for Sallie from its own folder. Nothing here is installed or turned on by this change.

Video editing for The BRIMM, run through Claude Code or Claude chat. Since 2026-10-14 it is a project of its own, apart from BRIMM Ops, the page that writes Sallie's drafts. Today it holds one tool, for unedited video, made for two Macs, the Mac Studio and Sallie's laptop, each with Final Cut Pro. A video dropped on a Mac that is switched to the light import gets its transcript and a cut plan on that Mac, with no Claude call. Sallie then says in her chat what it is, on one card, and Claude reads it once for its kind: Reel suggestions and spellings for a video finished by her editor, the cuts, notes and a rough cut for a raw recording, spellings for an iPhone clip, and, for a client or group session, only the teachings she asks for. Each ends in a hand-off for the BRIMM Ops page, but a session she keeps to its transcript only. On a Mac not switched, and in Jason's one-file run, the tool works as before: it writes a transcript and a cut plan, has Claude read the plan, and makes a rough cut, an upright version for Instagram, short clips and a Final Cut Pro project file. Everything it makes is a draft for a person to check. It never posts, sends or publishes anything.

Since 2026-10-25 the Mac Studio is switched: a video for it is dropped in `The BRIMM DB/BRIMM Video/Drop/Mac Studio/` in Dropbox and imported lightly into an episode of its own in `The BRIMM DB/BRIMM Video/Episodes/`. Sallie's laptop is not switched yet: its drop folder is still `The BRIMM DB/BRIMM Video/Unedited Video/Sallie's laptop/`, and it imports the old way until Jason switches it (`docs/setup/sallies-laptop.md`, "Switching her laptop"). See "Episodes" below.

What the project must do is in `docs/requirements.md`. Both Macs use Final Cut Pro (Jason, 2026-10-14). The design of the next version is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-14-brimm-video-next-version-design.md`, revised by `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-22-brimm-video-light-import-and-fork-design.md` (a light import, then a question in her chat). Its switch, parts L, H, F and D, is merged (`main` 1b43474) and turned on for the Mac Studio; among its later parts, still to come, are phase V (rendering and finishing without Final Cut, Reels made from the suggestions, fine edits by chat) and phase Z (Zoom downloads). One difference is this repo's own: `ffmpegArgs` in `tool/lib.js` takes an optional sound stream to map (2026-10-14, for an iPhone's APAC), and given none its arguments are those of brimm-operations' copy. ## Unedited video

A raw recording has retakes, false starts and long pauses in it. `tool/unedited.js` takes one and suggests what to cut, by rules. It then asks Claude to read that plan: Claude may change what is cut, writes notes for the person who edits the video, and picks short clips. From the plan the tool makes a rough cut, an upright version of the rough cut for Instagram, a Final Cut Pro project file with the cuts already made, and the short clips. It runs on the same Mac as the transcript tool, with the same programs and with ffprobe, which is installed with ffmpeg. Claude is asked through Claude Code (`claude`), which nothing here installs.

**What leaves the Mac.** The video and its sound never leave this Mac. When Claude is asked, the words of the transcript with their times and the rules' plan are sent to Claude through Claude Code, on the Claude account signed in on this Mac. The video's file name and its length go with them, and the folder it is in does not. With `--rules-only` Claude is never asked, and everything stays on the Mac. See "Claude's read" below.

What goes in: one unedited video. A sound file works too, and gets everything but the rough cut, the upright version, the Final Cut file and the clips.

What comes out, each file named after the video:

| File | What it is for |
|---|---|
| `<name>.srt`, `<name>.transcript.txt` | The transcript of the whole recording, retakes and all, on the recording's own timing. They are made as the transcript tool makes them, with its silence filter and its spelling |
| `<name>.words.json` | Every word with the second it starts and ends at, for whatever works on the recording next. The times are close and not exact: expect a tenth to a third of a second |
| `<name>.cut-plan.txt` | The cut plan to read. Its first lines say what could not be made, and whether Claude read the plan. A short clip that could not be cut is the one thing they do not name: the log and the end of the notes do. Then a line with the totals, and one line for each stretch that is kept or cut, with the reason for each cut. A cut of Claude's starts `CUT  Claude:` |
| `<name>.cut-plan.json` | The same plan to work from: the facts about the file, every phrase, and every range with its exact times, in seconds of the video. `pauses` lists the pauses ffmpeg heard, each as `[start, end]` in seconds of the video, the ones the plan was made from (kept since 2026-10-22, so that a reading made later, perhaps on the other Mac with no sound file, changes the plan with the same pauses; a plan from before has none, and a pause that never ends is written as lasting to the end of the video). `by` says who made it: `rules`, or `rules and Claude`. When Claude read the plan it also holds `claude`: the title it recommends for the whole video, when it gave one, the decisions as Claude gave them, its ideas for graphics as they were read (kept since 2026-10-14: `graphic … ideas` lists them, numbered, and `graphic … add --idea` puts one up), what was left out of its reply and why, and each clip with the names of the files made of it. When those decisions changed the plan it holds `changes` too, which lists what they changed. When Claude changed nothing there is no `changes`, and `by` is still `rules`. This file is written last, and is what says the video is done |
| `<name>.rough-cut.mp4` | The kept stretches joined, to watch. It is for judging the cuts and is not a final video: the picture 1080 lines at most. See "How the videos are written" below |
| `<name>.rough-cut.srt`, `<name>.rough-cut.transcript.txt` | The captions of the rough cut, and the same as plain text. This is the transcript of the video as it will be seen, without the retakes |
| `<name>.rough-cut.vertical.mp4` | The rough cut again, upright, for Instagram: 1080 by 1920, cropped on the speaker's face. Only a video that is wider than it is tall gets one. See "The upright version" below |
| `<name>.fcpxml` | A Final Cut Pro project file: the kept stretches cut together on a timeline, with the captions, a marker at each join, and a marker for each of Claude's ideas for a graphic. See "The Final Cut file" below |
| `<name>.edit-notes.txt` | Only when Claude read the plan. What Claude wrote for the editor: the title it recommends for the whole video, its notes, what it changed in the plan and why, its ideas for graphics, its clips, and at the end everything of its reply that could not be used |
| `<name>.reel-<n>.mp4` | Only when Claude picked clips. One short clip, whole, with what the plan cuts inside it left out. `<n>` is the clip's number in the notes. See "The short clips" below |
| `<name>.reel-<n>.srt` | The captions of that clip, on the clip's own clock |
| `<name>.reel-<n>.story-<k>.mp4` | Only for a clip of more than a minute: the clip again in parts of at most 60 seconds, for a Story |

One file by hand:

```
cd tool
node unedited.js "/path/to/raw video.mp4"
node unedited.js --rules-only "/path/to/raw video.mp4"    # never asks Claude
```

The files appear beside the video and nothing is moved. `--out "/another/folder"` writes them there. `node unedited.js --watch "/path/to/folder"` does every video directly in a folder that has no cut plan yet, and then files each one, with all of its files, in a new folder there named for the day, as the transcript tool files a video. A folder is for one of the two tools and not for both: whichever looks first files the video away. A video named `<something>.reel-<n>` or `<something>.reel-<n>.story-<k>` is taken for a clip the tool cut, as one named `<something>.rough-cut` is taken for a rough cut, and is never worked on. Doing a video again takes away the notes and the clips of the run before, with the rest of what that run made. Before 2026-10-14 a clip that a run was killed while writing was left beside the video under its name with `.writing` after it, and that is taken away too.

**How each file is written.** Every file is written whole in the run's own folder, among the Mac's temporary files, and only then moved where it belongs, under its own name, in one move. So a folder in Dropbox never sees a file half written, never uploads one, and never sees one renamed. Until 2026-10-14 the tool wrote each file beside the video under its name with `.writing` after it and renamed it when it was whole. Dropbox undid two of those renames that day, of videos of 387 and 70 MB it was most likely still uploading, and they stayed under their `.writing` names. On the Mac Studio the temporary files and Dropbox are on the same disk, so the move is one step. When they are not, as for `--out` on another disk, the file is copied there under its own name and deleted from the run's folder, and the log says so. After each move the tool looks for the file under its name, and the log says plainly when it is not there. That file then counts as not made.

- For one video, each file is moved beside the video, or into `--out`, as soon as it is whole. The cut plan comes last. With `--from-plan` only the videos are moved, each over the one made before.
- For a watched folder, the files are kept in the run's own folder until the video is done. Then the video is moved into the folder for the day, and each file goes straight in after it. The video is the one file renamed in the watched folder, and it has been there whole since before the run began. A run cut short leaves nothing in the watched folder but the video, which is done again the next time. When the video cannot be moved, its files go beside it. When a file cannot go into the folder for the day, it and the ones after it go into the watched folder instead, and the log says which.

Having the Mac watch a folder for unedited video: the installer sets that up. For one of the two Macs (`--machine`), which watches its own drop folder and imports episodes, see "Setting a Mac up" and "Switching a Mac's drop folder" under "Episodes" below. `<main>` is the main folder the transcript tool watches on the Mac Studio, in Dropbox:

The Mac then runs `node unedited.js --watch` on that folder whenever something lands in it, under the launch agent `com.thebrimm.unedited`. That is the label the agent had when the tool was in brimm-operations, so a run of this installer replaces that agent in place. What it did is in `~/Library/Logs/brimm-video.log`. More than one folder can be named, and the list replaces the last one, so name every folder each time. The installer installs ffmpeg, whisper-cpp and Node with Homebrew when they are missing, and downloads the transcriber's model once (about 1.6 GB) when it is not in `~/Library/Application Support/BRIMM Transcribe/`, where the transcript tool keeps it. It asks first, and `--yes` goes ahead without asking. It refuses a folder that the transcript tool's launch agent, `com.thebrimm.transcribe`, watches already: whichever tool looked first would file the video away. It reads that agent's file for the folders and never writes, loads or stops it. `./install.sh --remove` stops `com.thebrimm.unedited`, and takes away the setup's check of Claude, `com.thebrimm.claude-check`, if a setup left it behind (below); never the transcript tool's agent. The tool is run straight from this folder, so the branch checked out here is the tool: keep it on `main`, and work in a git worktree.

Add `--rules-only` to that line and the launch agent runs `node unedited.js --rules-only --watch`, so Claude is never asked. window, or its failing to open, changes nothing about the work. A video dropped for the other Mac shows its window on that

**The cuts are suggestions, made by rules and then by Claude, and are for a person to check.** They have only been tested on made-up speech so far, spoken by the Mac's own voice. The recording is first split into phrases: a phrase ends at a pause, which is 0.3 seconds or more of quiet, or at the end of a sentence. Then the rules, which are in `tool/cuts.js`:

- **A retake** is a phrase, or several in a row, that is said again straight after it: what comes next opens with the same four words or more, or shares at least 70% of the words, in order. The earlier telling is cut and the last one is kept. The second telling starts within 90 seconds, with no more than a spoken cue and a few words ("Sorry. Where was I?") between the two. The same words said again after other things is a point made twice, and both are kept.
- **A false start** is a phrase of two to six words that does not end a sentence, and whose words begin the phrase after it. A single word said twice across a pause ("Never," and then "never give up") is kept: it may be said twice on purpose.
- **A cue** is a phrase that only says to start over: "let me do that again", "take two", "one more time". It is cut, and so is the phrase before it when that phrase is then said again.
- **A pause** of a second and a half or more between two kept phrases is shortened to 0.4 seconds. The silence before the first kept words and after the last is cut. Only what ffmpeg heard as silent is cut this way. A sound between two pauses that the transcriber wrote no word for is kept, and each of the two pauses is shortened on its own: the sound may be a word the transcriber missed.

A phrase is only ever cut whole, and a cut is only ever made in a pause, a fifth of a second from the words that are kept. ffmpeg hears where the pauses are exactly. The transcriber's time for a word is only close. So where somebody runs straight on with no pause, nothing is cut. And where no pause is heard before the first words or after the last, as when music plays under them, the recording is kept from its start or to its end.

What to look for when checking a plan:

- Two sentences built the same way, one straight after the other ("The first thing you need is a clear offer. The second thing you need is a clear price."), can pass for a retake, and the first would be cut.
- After a pause the transcriber sometimes writes a sentence twice that was said once. The rules know such a line by its having no time to be said in, and cut nothing for it. A doubled line that was given time of its own would pass for a retake.
- The transcriber writes the same broken-off words now as "So the..." and now as "So the." With the full stop they pass for a sentence, and the false start is kept.
- A kept line with no words on it is a sound the transcriber wrote no word for: a cough, a door, or a word it missed. Beside a phrase that is cut, such a sound is cut with the phrase.
- The captions come from the pass that times the words, which is not quite the pass the transcript tool makes. On one made-up recording of five minutes the two differed by one caption, and by a few hundredths of a second here and there. On another this pass wrote a capital on every word, and 118 captions where the transcript tool's wrote 84.

Claude's read: once the rules have made a plan, and before anything is made from it, the tool asks Claude once to read it. What it asks, how the reply is read and how the plan is changed are in `tool/notes.js`.

- What Claude is given: the file's name and length, and every phrase of the plan on a line of its own, with the time it starts, what the rules decided for it and why, and its words. It is told that the footage is for the show, and who the host is. Nothing else is sent.
- What Claude is asked for, as one JSON object: decisions, only where it disagrees with the rules (an abandoned take the rules kept, a sentence that trails off, or something the rules cut that is a repeat for emphasis); up to 200 words of notes for the editor; up to six ideas for a graphic, each on a kept phrase; up to five clips of 15 to 90 seconds that would stand alone; and one title of at most eight words to put on screen for the whole video. A reply without a title is read as it was before the title was asked for, on 2026-10-14.
- What Claude cannot do: **it reads the words and never sees the picture or hears the sound.** It cannot tell which take looks or sounds better, and it is told so. The transcriber also mis-hears a word now and then. So every cut Claude adds, and every cut of the rules that it takes back, is for a person to check. Each is listed in `<name>.edit-notes.txt` with its reason, and `CUT  Claude:` marks its cuts in `<name>.cut-plan.txt`.
- What is done with its reply: a decision is only applied to a phrase the plan has, only at a pause, and never when it would leave nothing kept. Phrases with no pause between them are cut or kept together. The plan is then made again by the rules' own code, with the pauses ffmpeg heard, so a cut of Claude's falls where a cut of the rules would. That plan is the plan for everything after it: the rough cut and its captions, the upright version, the Final Cut file and the two plan files. What was left out of the reply, and why, is at the end of the notes and in `<name>.cut-plan.json`.
- How it is asked: the tool runs `claude -p --model claude-opus-5-5 --tools "" --strict-mcp-config --disable-slash-commands --no-session-persistence --setting-sources "" --output-format json`, in a new empty folder, with the request on its standard input. So Claude Code has none of its own tools and no skills, starts no tool server (MCP) that is set up for this user, reads no settings file and no project files, and saves no session. The words of a recording are a stranger’s text as far as Claude is concerned, so it is given nothing it could act with: all it can do is answer, and the tool reads that answer as data. It is given ten minutes. The tool looks for `claude` on the PATH, then in `/opt/homebrew/bin`, `/usr/local/bin` and `.local/bin` in the home folder.
- The model is named (`CLAUDE_MODEL` in `tool/unedited.js`), not left to the account's default, so that both Macs read a plan with the same one, and a change of default changes nothing. It was added on 2026-10-14; before that the account's default read every plan.
- That was asked once: on 2026-10-13, with these options, Claude was asked which tools it had and answered none. That is its own word and not a proof, which is why the options are given and not trusted to a default.
- What a call leaves on the Mac: Claude Code keeps a folder of its own for every folder it is run in, in `.claude/projects` in the home folder, named after that folder's path. Each call is run in a newly named folder, so each call leaves one more there, and a call that is tried twice leaves two. The one real run left `-private-var-folders-…-T-brimm-unedited-28031-1791927837336-claude-1`, with an empty folder named `memory` in it and nothing of the request or the reply. The tool takes away the folder it made for the call and leaves that one, because it deletes nothing under `.claude`. Such a folder holds nothing, and can be deleted by hand.
- What leaves the Mac: only that request, which is the words of the transcript with their times, the rules' plan, and the video's file name and length. So a name such as a client's, when it is in the file's name or is said on camera, is sent. It goes to Claude through Claude Code, on the Claude account signed in on this Mac, and counts against that account's use. The video and its sound never leave the Mac.
- When Claude cannot be reached: a call that ends badly, runs out of time, prints something that cannot be read, says it failed, or gives a reply nothing can be read from is tried once more, three seconds later. After two failures the rules' plan is used as it is. The log says why each try failed, the first lines of `<name>.cut-plan.txt` say `Claude could not be asked (the reason), so this plan is the rules' alone.`, and the banner says so. There are then no notes and no clips. This is not a failed run: everything else is made.
- When Claude Code is not on the Mac, Claude is not asked. The log says so once for the run, and the first lines of each `<name>.cut-plan.txt` say `Claude Code is not installed, so this plan is the rules' alone.`
- `--rules-only` never asks Claude. The log says so once for the run. Everything is then as the rules alone make it, and nothing leaves the Mac.
- Tried by hand on this Mac on 2026-10-13, before this was built: the call worked from a shell and from a launch agent, on the Claude account signed in here, with no key. The plan of a 58-second recording took 19 seconds. One call failed with "Failed to authenticate: OAuth session expired and could not be refreshed", and the next one worked. That is why a failed call is tried once more.

The short clips: for each clip Claude picked that can be cut from the plan, the tool cuts `<name>.reel-<n>.mp4`. It is the kept stretches from the clip's first phrase to its last, joined, with what the plan cuts between them left out.

- A clip of a video that is wider than it is tall is upright, 1080 by 1920, each stretch cropped on the speaker by the same stills and faces the upright version found. Without swift it is cropped at the centre of the picture, as the upright version is, and `<name>.cut-plan.txt` says so. A clip of a video that is upright or square already keeps its shape.
- **The clips have no captions burned into the picture.** The ffmpeg on this Mac has no filter that draws text (no `subtitles`, `ass` or `drawtext`). Each clip comes with `<name>.reel-<n>.srt` instead: its captions on the clip's own clock, to be added where the clip is posted or in an editor. The parts of a Story have no caption file of their own.
- A clip of more than a minute also comes as the parts of a Story, `<name>.reel-<n>.story-<k>.mp4`, each at most 60 seconds of what is kept. It is cut on the last pause in the 15 seconds before the 60 second mark, and at the mark itself when nobody pauses there. Each end of a part is moved to the nearest whole picture. A part that would then hold more pictures than 60 seconds do ends that many pictures sooner, and those pictures are in no part. Measured on 2026-10-13 with the real ffmpeg, on a test picture at 29.97 pictures a second: a part cut at the mark came out at 1799 pictures, 60.027 seconds, without this, and at 1798 pictures, 59.993 seconds, with it.
- A clip is cut whether or not it fits a Reel. One that is shorter than 15 seconds or longer than 90 has a line under it in the notes that says so. These are Instagram's limits as they were understood in October 2026.
- A clip is left out, and the notes say why, when it names a phrase the plan does not have, when nothing in it is kept, or when it takes a phrase a clip before it has. Five are made at most. A clip keeps the number of its place in Claude's reply, so the numbers in the file names can have gaps.
- A clip that starts or ends where there is no pause is cut on a word's own time, which is only close. The notes say to check its first or its last word.
- When one clip cannot be cut, the log and the notes say so and the others are cut all the same. The run then ends as one in which a file could not be made. `<name>.cut-plan.txt` and the banner do not name the clip.
- Each clip is one pass of ffmpeg over the whole video, as the upright version is, and so is each part of a Story. So the clips of a long recording can take several times as long as its upright version. Nobody has measured that on a long recording yet.

The Final Cut file: double-click `<name>.fcpxml`. Final Cut Pro opens it as a project named `<name> rough cut`, in an event named `Rough cuts`, in the library `BRIMM rough cuts`. That library is kept in the folder `BRIMM Video Work` in the home folder, because Final Cut Pro cannot keep a library in a folder that syncs to a cloud drive. On the Mac this was built on, the Movies folder is in Dropbox, and making a library there fails with "Select a local storage device". The file names its library, so Final Cut Pro asks nothing, and makes the library when it is not there. That was seen twice on 2026-10-13 with Final Cut Pro 12.4: of a file written by hand, and then of a file the tool wrote for a made-up recording, which made the library and put five pieces, their captions and a marker at each join on the timeline. The library is named as a `file://` address with no slash at its end. The tool makes the folder `BRIMM Video Work` when it is not there. It never opens Final Cut Pro.

What the file holds:

- The kept stretches of the plan as clips on a timeline, one after another, each end on a whole picture.
- The captions of the whole recording, from `<name>.srt`, each on the clip it is shown over.
- A marker on the first picture after each join. It says how much was taken out there and why, in the plan's own words: `Taken out, 6.9 seconds. Retake: said again at 0:10, the later one is kept`. So every cut can be checked where it was made. A cut of Claude's reads `Taken out, 2.7 seconds. Claude:` and then its reason.
- A marker for each of Claude's ideas for a graphic, at the start of the phrase the idea sits on: `Graphic idea: the three numbers on screen`. It is an idea and nothing more: no title or graphic is put on the timeline. An idea whose phrase is cut gets no marker, and the notes say what became of it.

What it cannot hold:

- The video. The file names the video by where it is on this Mac: beside the file, or for a watched folder in the dated folder the two are filed in. Once the video or that folder is moved or renamed, a file opened after that shows its clips as missing, until Final Cut Pro is told where the video is (File, Relink Files). And the file is for the Mac that made it: on another Mac the video is somewhere else.
- A title or a graphic: the import writes none (Claude's ideas are markers). They are added in a session (`text`, `graphic`, `logo`, a Reel's captions and title) and shown on the next push, as Final Cut's built-in Basic Title and Basic Lower Third, each line naming its font and size, and pictures from `media/`.
- An export. It cannot make Final Cut Pro write a video.
- Captions cut to fit. They are the whole recording's captions. One that lies wholly in a cut is left out, and one that runs over a join is shortened to its clip, but its words are never changed. So a caption beside a cut can hold words that were cut. `<name>.rough-cut.srt` has the captions of the kept words alone.

The file is checked before it is written. One that does not pass is not written, and `<name>.cut-plan.txt` says why.

The upright version: for a video that is wider than it is tall, `<name>.rough-cut.vertical.mp4` is the kept stretches of the rough cut again, cropped to 9:16 and made 1080 by 1920. The tool takes a still of the video every two seconds and finds the faces in them, on this Mac (`tool/faces.swift`, with Apple's Vision framework). Each kept stretch then gets one crop, the full height of the picture, with the largest face in its middle. A video that is already upright or square gets no such file, and the log says it needs none.

Its limits:

- One fixed crop for each kept stretch. It suits one speaker who stays seated. It does not follow a speaker who moves, and with two people in the picture it takes the larger face and does not know who is talking.
- A small source comes out soft. The crop of a 640x360 Zoom video is 202 pixels wide, and is made 1080 wide: more than five times its size.
- A stretch no face was found in takes the crop of the whole video, and the log says how many such stretches there were.
- It matches the rough cut picture for picture only when the video's pictures come at an even rate and its sound starts with its picture. The rough cut puts every picture on an even grid and the sound on the video's clock, and the upright version keeps the video's own pictures and sound as they are. So of a video whose pictures come unevenly, as a phone or Zoom may write them, or whose sound starts late, the upright version comes out a little longer than the rough cut, and `<name>.rough-cut.srt` runs ahead of it by the end. Measured on 2026-10-13 on made-up videos of two minutes with 40 cuts: 0.4 seconds longer when two pictures in every nine were missing, and 0.2 when the sound started half a second after the picture. At that rate an hour of talk with 250 cuts ends more than a second apart.
- The faces are found with `swift`, which comes with Xcode's command line tools (`xcode-select --install`). When swift is missing or fails, or what it prints cannot be read, every stretch is cropped at the centre of the picture, and the log and `<name>.cut-plan.txt` say so.

How the videos are written: the rough cut, the upright version and the short clips are H.264, made by x264 at a quality (`-crf`) and not at a fixed bitrate, so each is only as large as its picture needs. The rough cut is written at 23, x264's own default. An upright file is enlarged to 1080 by 1920, and an enlarged picture has no more detail than its source, so it is written at 23 and 2 more for each doubling: 25 from 1080 lines, 26 from 720, 28 from a 360-line Zoom recording. The sound is AAC at 48 kHz, and each file has its index at the front, so it can play before all of it is read. Until 2026-10-14 every file was written at a fixed 8 Mbit by the Mac's own encoder, with sound at whatever rate the recording had and the index at the end. A Zoom recording of 32 minutes, 640x360 at 0.67 Mbit and 182 MB, then came out as a rough cut of 1.32 GB, an upright version of 1.79 GB and clips of 40 to 78 MB. Measured on 2026-10-14 on a made-up recording like it, which came out at the same sizes the old way: a rough cut of 45 MB, an upright version of 156 MB, and clips of 3.5 to 6.8 MB, with the same pictures as before, and sound within 3 ms of where it was. x264 is slower than the Mac's own encoder: at 1080 lines it writes about 5 seconds of video a second, against 13, and the upright version of the made-up recording took 3.5 minutes, against 2.

Making the videos again: `node unedited.js "/path/to/raw video.mp4" --from-plan` makes the rough cut, its upright version, and each clip and part of a Story that the plan names as made, again from the `<name>.cut-plan.json` an earlier run wrote, as the tool writes videos now. Use it when only the way videos are written has changed and the cuts and clips are to stay, as on 2026-10-14: a full run would make a new transcript and ask Claude again, and Claude might pick other clips. No transcript is made and Claude is not asked. The faces are looked for again. Every other file stays as it was, the Final Cut file too, which names the video and none of these. A video that cannot be made keeps the one made before, and the log says so. It makes nothing when there is no plan, when the plan cannot be read, or when it is of another video, by its name or by the length, size and frame rate ffprobe gives now. It is for one video, never for a watched folder, and it never moves the video. With `--out`, the plan is read from that folder; `--out` anywhere in an episode's folder, or in Episodes itself (through a link, or in another case, alike), is refused, since an episode's videos go in by the rule.

When no transcript can be made, `<name>.failed.txt` says why, as the transcript tool's note does. When the transcript is made and the plan, the rough cut, the upright version, the Final Cut file or Claude's notes are not, what was made is kept, and the first lines of `<name>.cut-plan.txt` say what is missing and why, a line for each. When no plan can be made, Claude is not asked.

On some recordings the transcriber dies while it times the words, and does so every time that recording is tried. A review on 2026-10-13 saw it on one made-up recording of 62 minutes, out of about fifty tried. The transcript is then written by the transcript tool's own pass, which reads the same sound without trouble, and `<name>.cut-plan.txt` says that no cut plan could be made and why. Such a recording takes about twice as long.

**Writing into Dropbox: the rule.** Dropbox undid two renames on 2026-10-14, minutes after they had returned. So nothing in Dropbox is written into, renamed or moved. Every file is written whole on this Mac's own disk, then put into Dropbox under its final name as an APFS clone, by `/bin/cp -c`. `cp -c` makes a plain byte copy, without a word, when the two folders are on different disks (its manual says so), so the tool checks they are on one disk first, and where a clone cannot be made it stops and says why: nothing is copied byte by byte. A file already there is deleted first and the new one cloned in. Each file is looked at right after, and again at least 15 minutes after the import's last copy, the late check. A file missing or short then is put in again from its work copy. Only after the late check passes are the run's folder and the raw video in the drop folder deleted. Whether a clone into Dropbox's folder is safe where a rename was not has still to be seen by hand (the deployment note of the pull request that built this).

**A video arrives** when its size has settled and it is whole on this Mac: its blocks on the disk cover its size, or the disk does not mark it dataless. Dropbox can show a file at its full size before any of it is here, as it does for a video dropped from another device into a folder that is not kept offline, and ffprobe could then hang. Such a placeholder is read through once, which has Dropbox bring it down, and then looked at again. The read is python3's, from Xcode's command line tools, which first turns downloads on for itself alone: a program the launch agent starts has them off, and macOS refuses its read of a placeholder at once, with "Resource deadlock avoided". A read that keeps getting more of the file is waited for, however slowly; one that gets nothing for two minutes is given up, and the log says so. The detached run `open` starts brings an episode's files over the same way. This holds for every watched folder. So that a video comes down at once, mark the drop folder once in Finder: Control-click it and choose Make available offline. The installer says so.

**What the import does**, for a video in this Mac's drop folder (`tool/filing.js`):

1. It leaves alone a video an episode already holds (by its name and size), and one whose import is running on either Mac (at that size), failed, or found a problem at its late check. The log says why, once. A held video is looked at again each minute, so one whose failed status is taken away (`retry`) is imported. A status file this Mac's stopped run left is taken away, and the video imported again, and so are the folders stopped runs left in `.runs`.
2. It writes a status file in `Episodes/.importing/` at the start, at each step and once a minute: a new file each time, `<video> (<Mac>) <import id> <n>.json`, the one before deleted. The import id keeps two imports of one name apart.

An upright, square or short recording (under 90 seconds) is a short video: it gets no upright version and no Reels. An episode gets Reel candidates in place of the short clips: `reels.json`, and a small upright preview of each in `previews/` (see "Reel candidates" below). A video replaced in the drop folder while it is worked on, as by a corrected export of the same name, stops that import before anything is filed, and the new file is imported on its own.

## Reel candidates

Phase 3 of the design, built on 2026-10-14: Reels made light at the import, as Jason asked ("some of those may not be utilized, so the initial reels creation should be lightweight"). Only a Reel that is picked gets more.

**At the import** of an episode (not a short video), Claude's clips become Reel candidates. Claude picks up to five, each with words for the screen of at most eight words. `reels.json` holds them, each with its stretches of the raw video, each stretch's crop on the speaker's face, its title, why Claude picked it, its length and whether it fits a Reel, and its words. Each gets one preview, `previews/reel-<n>.mp4`: upright, 540 by 960, small (x264 `veryfast`, quality 30). No full-size clip is cut, and no Story part or caption file. When there are none, `reels.json` says why: a short video, a recording with no picture, Claude not asked (the import went by the rules alone, or Claude Code was not on the Mac it names), Claude not reachable, no cut plan, or nothing that stands alone. `reels` says whether Claude can be asked again as the Mac that shows the list finds it now, in the words `ask` would refuse with.

**How a preview is made** (`tool/reels.js`, `previewSteps`). Each stretch is read alone, by seeking to it, so the recording is never read whole: the old clips read it from its start, up to 35 seconds for one clip near the end of an hour of 1080 lines. Each stretch's picture goes on an even grid from the seek time and holds exactly its whole pictures, and its sound is on the same clock. A preview is made in parts of at most 8 stretches (2 for a 4K picture), each part with its sound kept whole, and the parts are joined with the picture copied and the sound written once. So its memory does not grow with its stretches. Every ffmpeg runs under the watchdog (`tool/guard.js`): in a process group of its own, its memory looked at every 50 ms, and the whole group killed past 2 GB or past its time (60 seconds, and 5 more for every second of the preview). The watchdog also kills it when the import itself is stopped (SIGTERM, as launchctl does, SIGINT or SIGHUP). A stopped preview's candidate says it could not be made, and why. On 2026-10-14 a preview of stretches past the end of the file grew without end and took the Mac Studio to 101 GB before it was killed by hand: stretches are now stopped at the end of the video, each stretch's picture and sound are read apart, and the watchdog is the third guard.

Measured on the Mac Studio with ffmpeg 9.0.2, on made-up recordings only:
- a preview takes 1 to 1.6 seconds and is about 2 MB, wherever it is in the recording;
- 60 stretches of 1080 lines peaked at 553 MB, 100 at 536 MB, and 40 of 3840x2160 at 713 MB;
- picture counts were exact at 25, 30, 29.97 and 23.976 pictures a second, with no silent holes;
- on a clip with dropped pictures and freezes, each flash came with its beep.

**A picked Reel's project** (`tool/reel.js`). Picking a Reel writes its upright Final Cut project, `edit/reel-<n>.v0.fcpxml`, by the rule. Each push makes a new version of it, in the episode's one event. The project is 1080 by 1920. Its clips are the candidate's stretches of the raw video, each filled into the frame and moved onto the face by its crop. A marker sits at each join, saying what was taken out there and why, and on each stretch whose crop is a guess (no face was found). The raw video's sound and colour go on its asset, an iPhone's HDR colour space included. What phase 0 settled with Final Cut Pro 12.4:
- each clip must name the wide video's own format, or Final Cut takes it for upright and does not fill;
- with that, fill is honoured;
- a move is in hundredths of the frame's height, and a positive x moves the picture right;
- the 1080x1920 format has no name and the colour space Rec. 709.

Captions and the title come in phase 4.

**Asking again** (`ask`). Claude is asked with the import's own request and call, of the cut plan the import wrote, and only its clips are taken. A Reel already picked or finished keeps its number, its stretch and its preview. A new clip that shares words with one is left out, and the result says so. New candidates are numbered on from the highest number ever given, so a number never names two stretches, and no candidate is ever taken out of `reels.json`. When Claude cannot be asked, or the episode changed meanwhile, nothing is changed; and when a file cannot be put in, or the record cannot be written, every file is put back as it was. Once the other Mac has the episode open (its claim), a late check copies nothing into it: each file not as this Mac put it is kept in `put back/` and named, unless it has a conflicted copy in the episode (then it is only named, for a person to choose, since one side is in the copy) or the claim's own record lists the other Mac's version of it (only noted). | "Reel 2 says Say it again, better" | `title "<episode>" reel <n> --words "<words>"` | Sets a Reel's title on screen: the words in its picture, which the hand-off gives BRIMM Ops. It starts as Claude's hook |
| "Show me the Reels" | `reels "<episode>" [--no-play]` | Lists the Reel candidates, and plays each preview in QuickTime Player. For a video she has answered: its suggestions with times on the clock she is reading, and nothing opens; then that brimm-video cannot make Reels from it yet, and that for now she cuts them in OpusClip and BRIMM Ops writes their post text |
| "Ask Claude again for Reels" | `ask "<episode>"` | Asks Claude again for candidates, detached. Picked Reels stay as they are. A video she has answered is read again as its kind, as `kind … --again` does: its earlier reading's files, its hand-off and the text files beside it in `final/` are set aside into `history/` first (its final videos and exports stay; answered as a session, every file of `final/` goes), a raw video starts again from the rules' plan, and a session answered so gets an empty session revision of its hand-off at once |
| "Finish Reels 1 and 3" / "Drop 2" | `reel pick\|drop "<episode>" <n>…` | Records which Reels are finished, and writes each picked Reel's upright project as `edit/reel-<n>.v0.fcpxml`. A finished Reel is neither picked nor dropped again. A suggestion of a video she has answered is not picked: it says brimm-video cannot make Reels from this video yet, naming each, with OpusClip and BRIMM Ops as above; `push … reel <n>` and every change on such a Reel say the same |
| "Show reel 1 in Final Cut" | `push "<episode>" reel <n>` | Pushes a picked Reel's upright project, through the same push as the main video, with its question about hand edits. For an episode imported before phase 3 it is still the clip the import cut, whole, with its captions |

**For each part, in this order:** Share it into `From Final Cut` (check the save dialog says so), and wait for Final Cut's notice that the Share is done. Then click in its timeline, choose File > Export XML…, and save it into `From Final Cut`. The export keeps the hand edits: a Final Cut library is not backed up. Then say "finish the episode".

**What `finish` says and asks.** It finds each part's files by the project names recorded each time the part was shown in Final Cut, never by the episode's current name, in `From Final Cut` and in what the other Mac released into the episode together, and takes the Share of the newest version. It says what each part is taken from. It asks:

- which file is final, when two different files are of the newest version (`--take "main=<file>"`), saying where each is when one name is both on this Mac and from the other Mac (`--take "main=the other Mac"`, or `this Mac`);
- about a picked Reel with no Share: Share it, or leave it out (`--without "reel 2"`);
- for File > Export XML… of a part whose export is older than its Share, and of a part never exported, unless there were no hand edits since it was shown (`--no-hand-edits "reel 2"`);
- about a Share that Final Cut renamed ("… 1"), which it never takes;
- what a finished Reel's title on screen says, when it has none, naming the Reel by its number and its first words. BRIMM Ops takes that title for the words in the picture, so none is ever made up.

A finished video, and an iPhone clip she answered, are not finished in Final Cut: their hand-off is written from the spellings card ("show me the spellings"), so `finish` says that, or that the hand-off is written already as revision N, or, on a Mac that hands off version 1 (which has no finished video), that it waits for version 2, before it looks for any part. A raw video answered so since the switch (kind "raw") is written into a version 1 hand-off as an episode. In version 1 it refuses an episode whose name is still a placeholder and an episode without an approved title (a short video needs none); version 2 needs a name only for a raw video, and no title. In both it refuses a file of the episode that is not whole in Dropbox, a finish already running, and an episode the other Mac has open. With `--yes` and nothing left to ask, it claims for this Mac an episode nobody has open, so that the other Mac's `open` is refused while the finish runs, then starts the rest detached and returns: "Started. The panel shows how far it is." The finish lets that claim go when it ends or fails. "How far is it?" (`status`) says what it made, what failed, and what it offers.

**What it does then, detached,** holding the episode's lock and keeping the Mac awake:

1. Waits until each finished video is whole: its size the same twice, five seconds apart, and ffprobe able to read it. After ten minutes it stops: "… is still being written by Final Cut. Say finish again when its Share is done."
2. Checks each video's shape: a Reel is upright, and the main video has the raw video's shape. A wrong shape stops the finish, naming the file. A video whose first words are not those of its part is named for a person to check by eye, and the finish goes on: a trim at the start changes them.
3. Transcribes each video again, as the transcript tool does: its own pass writes the captions, with the silence filter and the show's and the episode's spellings, and the word pass writes a time for every word. When the word pass dies, the captions alone are used, and the result says the word times are missing.
4. Cuts each finished Reel of more than a minute into the parts of a Story, each at most 60 seconds: on the last pause in the 15 seconds before each minute, else in the widest gap between words there, as when music plays under the voice, else at the minute.
5. Writes the hand-off and copies everything into the episode's `final/` folder by the rule for writing into Dropbox: each video under the name Final Cut gave it, its `.srt`, `.transcript.txt` and `.words.json`, its Story parts, its export, and the hand-off last.
6. Writes the record: the finish, each part's file and the export that keeps its edit (or that it had no hand edits), and each Reel finished. It shows the hand-off in Finder, to drop on the BRIMM Ops page.
7. At least 15 minutes after the last copy, checks the copies again. Only when each is whole does it delete from `From Final Cut` the files it took from there.

**The hand-off**, `final/<name>.brimm-ops.json`, version 1: the episode's id, kind, name as given, the day it was recorded, and its approved title; the main video's file, length and captions; and each finished Reel's number, title, file, length and captions, with where its words fall in the finished main video when at least 90 in 100 of them are found there in order. A second `finish` transcribes only what changed, keeps every other part as the last one made it, and raises the hand-off's `revision`, as `spell` does when it makes the hand-off again: one above the record's last finish and every hand-off of the episode's id in its `final/` and set aside into its `history/`, since one set aside is still one the page may hold (counting `history/` came with version 2's work, with the settings unchanged: it can only raise the number). The contract with BRIMM Ops is `tool/tests/fixtures/handoff-v1.json` and `handoff-v1-short-video.json`, which brimm-operations keeps copies of: change neither without changing both.

**Version 2**, once this Mac's settings say so (`node tool/settings.js set --handoff 2`), is what `finish` and `spell` write for every kind, an episode imported before the switch as raw (`tool/handoff-v2.js`, and its contract `tool/tests/fixtures/handoff-v2-*.json`): no title, since titles live on the BRIMM Ops page, so `finish` needs none, and a name only for a raw video; a raw or short video's name titles as `main.onPicture`; and each Reel's `fromSuggestion`. A new version keeps its first version's id, and each hand-off's `revision` is above every one written for that id on this Mac, in either version, in every version's `final/` and `history/` and those of an episode whose record this Mac cannot read yet; other videos' folders, which may be online-only, are not read. When the chain of versions cannot be followed to the first (one not yet brought over by Dropbox, or two that each name the other), `finish` and `spell` refuse, naming the video as every command names it (a video not answered, or a session, by its label), and write nothing, `finish` at once. So they do when another version of the video, earlier or later, is a client or group session whose mark is not cleared (or that has no mark), naming the video as every command names it: its hand-off would be under the session's id, above the session's revision, and bring back the words the page withdrew. A number given to a suggestion or a Reel is its identity on the page and never repeats within a video, so `finish` and `spell` write nothing, and say so, when the hand-off would give a number again that a hand-off of another version of the video gave under the same id (its own earlier revisions give their numbers by right); `highestNumberOf` in `tool/finish.js` is the highest ever given, for a new reading to number on from. A video's id never changes on the page either: when its own `final/` or `history/` holds a version 2 hand-off under another id than the one its versions lead to now (as after an answer linked it to an earlier video since), `finish` and `spell` refuse, saying both ids, and write nothing. `finish` also refuses, in either version and at once, before it looks in any folder, a client or group session (its own finish is the hand-off of the teachings she approved, above, and none of its transcripts may be in the shared folder) and a video not yet answered, naming it only by its label, as every command names such a video; `spell` refuses them so too once a video is finished, before it names or reads any file in `final/`. A version 2 hand-off is never made again as version 1: once one is on file for a video, `finish` and `spell` write version 2 for it, whatever the setting says.

**Afterwards**, the result offers to make the episode's folder online-only on this Mac (in Finder, Control-click it and choose Make online-only), and says how to free Final Cut's render files (in Final Cut, File > Delete Generated Library Files). Deleting an episode's whole Final Cut library by command comes in a later version: `free "<episode>" library` says so, and deletes nothing.

**Known limits.** Story parts and word times of an earlier cut stay in `final/` after a part is Shared again: the record no longer lists them, and nothing reads them back. A Share replaced by a file of the same size whose time was set back is taken for the one finish took. Without word times (when the transcriber's word pass died), where a Reel falls in the main video can be a second or so off when its parts lie apart there. When the other Mac takes the episode over while a finish copies into `final/`, the finish is not written into the record, but the record in force is written to list `final/`'s files at the sizes they have, with no finish in it: `final/` then mixes two finishes, so the next finish makes every part again.

**Not tried yet.** No real episode has been finished. Every part of `finish` has met stand-ins for ffprobe, ffmpeg and the transcriber only, on a made-up recording.
