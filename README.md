# BRIMM Video Studio

> **This repository is BRIMM Video Studio, the next version of brimm-video.** The HyperFrames Mac app will do the editing; Studio does everything around it: it takes a video in, reads it, makes the app's project, takes the exports back and writes BRIMM Ops its hand-off file. The architecture and the workflows are in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-27-brimm-video-studio-architecture.md`, and the build, step by step, in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-27-brimm-video-studio-build-plan.md`. Until those steps are merged, the code and everything below describe brimm-video as it was when this repository began (the tag `brimm-video-v1` and the late-check groundwork merged after it). Since 2026-11-03 the Mac Studio runs Studio, from this repo's main checkout. brimm-video itself keeps running on Sallie's laptop, from its own folder, until her laptop is moved to Studio.

Video editing for The BRIMM, run through Claude Code or Claude chat. Since 2026-10-14 it is a project of its own, apart from BRIMM Ops, the page that writes Sallie's drafts. Today it holds one tool, for unedited video, made for two Macs, the Mac Studio and Sallie's laptop, each editing in the HyperFrames app. A video dropped on a Mac gets its transcript and a cut plan on that Mac, with no Claude call (the light import, the only import since Phase 2). Sallie then says in her chat what it is, on one card, and Claude reads it once for its kind: Reel suggestions and spellings for a video finished by her editor, the cuts, notes and a rough cut for a raw recording, spellings, a line on what it is and up to three graphic cues for an iPhone clip, and, for a client or group session, only the teachings she asks for. Each ends in a hand-off for the BRIMM Ops page, but a session she keeps to its transcript only. In Jason's one-file run, and in any other folder it is told to watch, the import is the same: a transcript and a cut plan, and no video. Everything it makes is a draft for a person to check. It never posts, sends or publishes anything.

Since 2026-10-25 the Mac Studio is switched: a video for it is dropped in `The BRIMM DB/BRIMM Video/Drop/Mac Studio/` in Dropbox and imported lightly into an episode of its own in `The BRIMM DB/BRIMM Video/Episodes/`. Sallie's laptop was switched the same way on 2026-10-26, at brimm-video 7aaa110 (`docs/setup/sallies-laptop.md`, "Switching her laptop"): its drop folder is `The BRIMM DB/BRIMM Video/Drop/Sallie's laptop/`, and her old folder in `Unedited Video/` is gone. The Mac Studio's old folder there, `Unedited Video/Mac Studio/`, is no longer watched, and is for Jason to delete by hand once he has checked it is empty. See "Episodes" below.

What the project must do is in `docs/requirements.md`. Both Macs edit in the HyperFrames app. Studio retired Final Cut on 2026-11-06 (Jason: "Retire it now"); Final Cut can still open the app's MOV export by hand. The design of the next version is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-14-brimm-video-next-version-design.md`, revised by `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-22-brimm-video-light-import-and-fork-design.md` (a light import, then a question in her chat). Its switch, parts L, H, F and D, is merged (`main` 1b43474) and turned on for the Mac Studio; among its later parts, still to come, is phase Z (Zoom downloads). Setting Sallie's laptop up is `docs/setup/sallies-laptop.md`, which still describes brimm-video; moving it to Studio is checks 40 to 48 on the Studio By-Hand Checks page (https://claude.ai/artifact/TmFpCY7GfR1KWw9hcftzrT). ## A home of its own, for by-hand checks

`BRIMM_VIDEO_HOME` is for by-hand checks only: it gives Studio a home of its own, a scratch folder, so that a check runs as a Mac of its own. Studio keeps its own folders there (its settings, its work folder, the Downloads it looks in, its runs, pendings, progress and log), and `HOME` stays real, so `claude`, the transcription model, ffmpeg and the HyperFrames app run as they always do. Changing `HOME` itself breaks the login keychain and the model's place. It must be a whole path, or the command ends with 2 and says so. It is never set on a Mac in use, and Claude in Sallie's folder cannot set it: the guard allows only the one spelling of each command.

## Copies kept in step by hand

The tool borrows from two places in brimm-operations, and keeps its own copies. Each copy's first line names the file it was copied from and the commit, 84a2f61.

- `tool/shared/text.js`, `tool/shared/answers.js` and `tool/shared/transcript.js` are BRIMM Ops' `app/src/` files of those names, unchanged. `transcript.js` is how BRIMM Ops reads an `.srt`, so the tool's `.transcript.txt` is the same text BRIMM Ops makes of its `.srt`.
- `tool/shared/show.js` holds the show's name and the host's, the two lines of BRIMM Ops' `app/src/instructions.js` that the request to Claude names.
- `tool/lib.js` is the part of the transcript tool's `transcribe/lib.js` that this tool uses: which files it takes, what it asks ffmpeg and the transcriber to do, and how it reads what they wrote. The parts about Zoom calls are left out.
- `tool/run.js` is the part of `transcribe/transcribe.js` that this tool uses: how a program is run, where the programs are, and the lock on a watched folder. The lock is named as the transcript tool names it, so the two tools never work on one folder at the same time.

When one of those files changes in brimm-operations, change its copy here by hand. One difference is this repo's own: `ffmpegArgs` in `tool/lib.js` takes an optional sound stream to map (2026-10-14, for an iPhone's APAC), and given none its arguments are those of brimm-operations' copy. The transcript tool will want the same once it moves here (phase T).

## Unedited video

A raw recording has retakes, false starts and long pauses in it. `tool/unedited.js` takes one and suggests what to cut, by rules. That is the whole of the import, and it is the same for every video it takes (the light import; since Phase 2, "Final Cut retired", there is no other): a video dropped in a Mac's drop folder, a video in any other folder it is told to watch, and a single file given to it by hand. It makes the transcript, the words with their times, the rules' cut plan and the captions of the rough cut worked out from that plan. It asks Claude nothing and makes no video. It runs on the same Mac as the transcript tool, with the same programs and with ffprobe, which is installed with ffmpeg.

**What leaves the Mac.** Nothing of a video goes to Claude when it is imported, and the video and its sound never leave this Mac. Claude reads a video later, in her chat, once her answer says what it is (see "Episodes" below); that reading is where the transcript's words with their times, the plan and the file's name and length are sent, through Claude Code (`claude`), which nothing here installs. `--rules-only` is still accepted, and means nothing now.

What goes in: one unedited video. A sound file works too, and gets a transcript, its words, a plan and the rough cut's captions (a rough cut's captions need no picture). A video with a picture and no sound is filed alone from a drop folder (footage for a planned video) and fails anywhere else.

What comes out, each file named after the video:

| File | What it is for |
|---|---|
| `<name>.srt`, `<name>.transcript.txt` | The transcript of the whole recording, retakes and all, on the recording's own timing. They are made as the transcript tool makes them, with its silence filter and its spelling |
| `<name>.words.json` | Every word with the second it starts and ends at, for whatever works on the recording next. The times are close and not exact: expect a tenth to a third of a second |
| `<name>.cut-plan.txt` | The cut plan to read. Its first lines say that its times are the recording's own and that the plan is the rules' alone, or why no plan could be made. Then a line with the totals, and one line for each stretch that is kept or cut, with the reason for each cut |
| `<name>.cut-plan.json` | The same plan to work from: the facts about the file, every phrase, and every range with its exact times, in seconds of the video. `pauses` lists the pauses ffmpeg heard, each as `[start, end]` in seconds of the video, the ones the plan was made from, so that a reading made later, perhaps on the other Mac with no sound file, changes the plan with the same pauses (a pause that never ends is written as lasting to the end of the video). `by` says who made it: `rules`. A plan an older run wrote may also hold `claude`, `graphics` and `changes`, which are still read. This file is written last, and is what says the video is done |
| `<name>.rough-cut.srt`, `<name>.rough-cut.transcript.txt` | The captions of the rough cut, and the same as plain text, placed by the plan's own arithmetic. This is the transcript of the video as it will be seen, without the retakes. No rough cut is made at the import: it is made when a raw video is read (`tool/reading.js`) |

The import makes no rough cut, upright version or short clip, and writes no notes for an editor and no `reels.json`. Those files may still be there, beside a video that an older run made: the import of a video it does again takes away the ones that are named after it, with the rest of what that run made.

One file by hand:

```
cd tool
node unedited.js "/path/to/raw video.mp4"
```

The files appear beside the video and nothing is moved. `--out "/another/folder"` writes them there. `node unedited.js --watch "/path/to/folder"` does every video directly in a folder that has no cut plan yet, and then files each one, with all of its files, in a new folder there named for the day, as the transcript tool files a video. A folder is for one of the two tools and not for both: whichever looks first files the video away. A video named `<something>.reel-<n>` or `<something>.reel-<n>.story-<k>` is taken for a clip an older run cut, as one named `<something>.rough-cut` is taken for a rough cut, and is never worked on. The video of an episode in a drop folder is neither of those: see "Episodes" below. `--from-plan` is gone, since the import makes no videos now: asked for, the tool says `--from-plan is gone: the import makes no videos now.` and ends with 2, whatever else it was asked.

**How each file is written.** Every file is written whole in the run's own folder, among the Mac's temporary files, and only then moved where it belongs, under its own name, in one move. So a folder in Dropbox never sees a file half written, never uploads one, and never sees one renamed. Until 2026-10-14 the tool wrote each file beside the video under its name with `.writing` after it and renamed it when it was whole. Dropbox undid two of those renames that day, of videos of 387 and 70 MB it was most likely still uploading, and they stayed under their `.writing` names. On the Mac Studio the temporary files and Dropbox are on the same disk, so the move is one step. When they are not, as for `--out` on another disk, the file is copied there under its own name and deleted from the run's folder, and the log says so. After each move the tool looks for the file under its name, and the log says plainly when it is not there. That file then counts as not made.

- For one video, each file is moved beside the video, or into `--out`, as soon as it is whole. The cut plan comes last.
- For a watched folder, the files are kept in the run's own folder until the video is done. Then the video is moved into the folder for the day, and each file goes straight in after it. The video is the one file renamed in the watched folder, and it has been there whole since before the run began. A run cut short leaves nothing in the watched folder but the video, which is done again the next time. When the video cannot be moved, its files go beside it. When a file cannot go into the folder for the day, it and the ones after it go into the watched folder instead, and the log says which.
- A file that cannot be written ends the run of that video with the error in the log and no plan, and the video is done again the next time; in a drop folder it fails the import, and its status says why.

Having the Mac watch a folder for unedited video: the installer sets that up. For one of the two Macs (`--machine`), which watches its own drop folder and imports episodes, see "Setting a Mac up" and "Switching a Mac's drop folder" under "Episodes" below. `<main>` is the main folder the transcript tool watches on the Mac Studio, in Dropbox:

```
cd tool
./install.sh --print "<main>/Unedited Video Transfer"    # shows what it would do and changes nothing
./install.sh "<main>/Unedited Video Transfer"
```

The Mac then runs `node unedited.js --watch` on that folder whenever something lands in it, under the launch agent `com.thebrimm.unedited`. That is the label the agent had when the tool was in brimm-operations, so a run of this installer replaces that agent in place. What it did is in `~/Library/Logs/brimm-video.log`. More than one folder can be named, and the list replaces the last one, so name every folder each time. The installer installs ffmpeg, whisper-cpp and Node with Homebrew when they are missing, and downloads the transcriber's model once (about 1.6 GB) when it is not in `~/Library/Application Support/BRIMM Transcribe/`, where the transcript tool keeps it. It asks first, and `--yes` goes ahead without asking. It refuses a folder that the transcript tool's launch agent, `com.thebrimm.transcribe`, watches already: whichever tool looked first would file the video away. It reads that agent's file for the folders and never writes, loads or stops it. `./install.sh --remove` stops `com.thebrimm.unedited`, and takes away the setup's check of Claude, `com.thebrimm.claude-check`, if a setup left it behind (below); never the transcript tool's agent. The tool is run straight from this folder, so the branch checked out here is the tool: keep it on `main`, and work in a git worktree.

Add `--rules-only` to that line and the launch agent runs `node unedited.js --rules-only --watch`. The import never asks Claude, with it or without, so for the import it changes nothing (old launch agents still pass it, and it is still accepted). What Claude does read is a video once its person has said what it is (the words with their times, the plan and the video's file name and length, never a session's), and with `--machine`, `--rules-only` writes `readings: false` into this Mac's settings so that those readings ask Claude nothing. The installer's summary says whether it found Claude Code where the launch agent will look for it, and what is sent then. The installer does not install Claude Code and does not sign in to it. To have Claude asked again to read a video, run the line again without `--rules-only`, and turn the readings on; with `--machine`, `--rules-only` wrote `readings: false` into this Mac's settings, and a run without it leaves that as it is: turn the readings on with `node settings.js set --readings on` (folder mode has no readings at all). `./install.sh --import full` and `node settings.js set --import full` (and `plan` and `write` told it) are refused with `The full import is gone: this Mac imports lightly.`, end with 2 and change nothing; `--import light` is accepted. **The cuts are suggestions, made by rules, and are for a person to check.** They have only been tested on made-up speech so far, spoken by the Mac's own voice. The recording is first split into phrases: a phrase ends at a pause, which is 0.3 seconds or more of quiet, or at the end of a sentence. Then the rules, which are in `tool/cuts.js`:

- **A retake** is a phrase, or several in a row, that is said again straight after it: what comes next opens with the same four words or more, or shares at least 70% of the words, in order. The earlier telling is cut and the last one is kept. The second telling starts within 90 seconds, with no more than a spoken cue and a few words ("Sorry. Where was I?") between the two. The same words said again after other things is a point made twice, and both are kept.
- **A false start** is a phrase of two to six words that does not end a sentence, and whose words begin the phrase after it. A single word said twice across a pause ("Never," and then "never give up") is kept: it may be said twice on purpose.
- **A cue** is a phrase that only says to start over: "let me do that again", "take two", "one more time". It is cut, and so is the phrase before it when that phrase is then said again.
- **A pause** of a second and a half or more between two kept phrases is shortened to 0.4 seconds. The silence before the first kept words and after the last is cut. Only what ffmpeg heard as silent is cut this way. A sound between two pauses that the transcriber wrote no word for is kept, and each of the two pauses is shortened on its own: the sound may be a word the transcriber missed.

A phrase is only ever cut whole, and a cut is only ever made in a pause, a fifth of a second from the words that are kept. ffmpeg hears where the pauses are exactly. The transcriber's time for a word is only close. So where somebody runs straight on with no pause, nothing is cut. And where no pause is heard before the first words or after the last, as when music plays under them, the recording is kept from its start or to its end.

What to look for when checking a plan:

- Two sentences built the same way, one straight after the other ("The first thing you need is a clear offer. The second thing you need is a clear price."), can pass for a retake, and the first would be cut.
- After a pause the transcriber sometimes writes a sentence twice that was said once. The rules know such a line by its having no time to be said in, and cut nothing for it. A doubled line that was given time of its own would pass for a retake.
- The transcriber writes the same broken-off words now as "So the..." and now as "So the." With the full stop they pass for a sentence, and the false start is kept.
- A kept line with no words on it is a sound the transcriber wrote no word for: a cough, a door, or a word it missed. Beside a phrase that is cut, such a sound is cut with the phrase.
- The captions come from the pass that times the words, which is not quite the pass the transcript tool makes. On one made-up recording of five minutes the two differed by one caption, and by a few hundredths of a second here and there. On another this pass wrote a capital on every word, and 118 captions where the transcript tool's wrote 84.

**Claude's read is not part of the import.** Claude is asked to read a plan when a video is read in her chat (`tool/reading.js`) and when she asks for Reels again (`ask`): what it is asked, how the reply is read and how the plan is changed are in `tool/notes.js`, and the call is the one `tool/unedited.js` makes (`askOnce`): `claude -p --model claude-opus-5-5 --tools "" --strict-mcp-config --disable-slash-commands --no-session-persistence --setting-sources "" --output-format json`, in a new empty folder, with the request on its standard input, so that Claude Code has none of its own tools and no skills, starts no tool server (MCP) that is set up for this user, reads no settings file and no project files, and saves no session. The model is named (`CLAUDE_MODEL`), not left to the account's default, so that both Macs read a plan with the same one. Each call leaves a folder of its own under `.claude/projects` in the home folder, with an empty folder named `memory` in it and nothing of the request or the reply; the tool deletes nothing under `.claude`. `node unedited.js --check-claude` asks Claude once, to see that it can be asked from here: the installer runs it from a launch agent of its own.

A video that is wider than it is tall, an upright one, a short one and an iPhone's keep all the same plan; none gets an upright version at the import.

When no transcript can be made, `<name>.failed.txt` says why, as the transcript tool's note does (an episode's status says why instead). When the transcript is made and no plan is, the transcript is kept, `<name>.cut-plan.txt` says that no cut plan could be made and why, and the run ends as one in which a file could not be made.

On some recordings the transcriber dies while it times the words, and does so every time that recording is tried. A review on 2026-10-13 saw it on one made-up recording of 62 minutes, out of about fifty tried. The transcript is then written by the transcript tool's own pass, which reads the same sound without trouble, and `<name>.cut-plan.txt` says that no cut plan could be made and why. Such a recording takes about twice as long.

**None of this has been tried on real footage in this form.** The rules have only met made-up recordings, spoken by the Mac's own voice, and real drops on the Mac Studio since the light import was switched on there. A number given to a suggestion or a Reel is its identity on the page and never repeats within a video, so `finish` and `spell` write nothing, and say so, when the hand-off would give a number again that a hand-off of another version of the video gave under the same id (its own earlier revisions give their numbers by right); `highestNumberOf` in `tool/finish.js` is the highest ever given, for a new reading to number on from. A video's id never changes on the page either: when its own `final/` or `history/` holds a version 2 hand-off under another id than the one its versions lead to now (as after an answer linked it to an earlier video since), `finish` and `spell` refuse, saying both ids, and write nothing. `finish` also refuses, in either version and at once, before it looks in any folder, a client or group session (its own finish is the hand-off of the teachings she approved, above, and none of its transcripts may be in the shared folder) and a video not yet answered, naming it only by its label, as every command names such a video; `spell` refuses them so too once a video is finished, before it names or reads any file in `final/`. A version 2 hand-off is never made again as version 1: once one is on file for a video, `finish` and `spell` write version 2 for it, whatever the setting says.

**Afterwards**, for a video in Dropbox (one from before the switch), the result offers to make the episode's folder online-only on this Mac (in Finder, Control-click it and choose Make online-only). (`free "<episode>"` gives back a HyperFrames episode's working copy and renders: see its row above.)

**Known limits.** Story parts and word times of an earlier cut stay in `final/` after a part is exported again: the record no longer lists them, and nothing reads them back. An export replaced by a file of the same size whose time was set back is taken for the one finish took. Without word times (when the transcriber's word pass died), where a Reel falls in the main video can be a second or so off when its parts lie apart there. When the other Mac takes the episode over while a finish copies into `final/`, the finish is not written into the record, but the record in force is written to list `final/`'s files at the sizes they have, with no finish in it: `final/` then mixes two finishes, so the next finish makes every part again.
